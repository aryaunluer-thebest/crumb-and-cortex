Memory

Apple Cinnamon Muffins

Why one whiff of cinnamon can pull you straight back into a specific autumn years ago.

Read the science

Smell is wired differently from our other senses. Signals from the nose reach brain areas involved in emotion (the amygdala) and memory (the hippocampus) with fewer relay stops than sight or sound. That's one reason memories triggered by smell often feel unusually vivid and emotional.

Further reading: Rachel Herz's research on odor-evoked memory.

Reward

Spiced Pumpkin Bread

Why waiting for bread to come out of the oven can be almost as exciting as eating it.

Read the science

Dopamine is often called the "pleasure chemical," but researchers have found it's more closely tied to wanting and anticipation than to the enjoyment itself. The smell drifting from the oven builds that anticipation before you ever take a bite.

Further reading: Kent Berridge and Terry Robinson on "wanting" vs. "liking."

Focus

Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

Why measuring, stirring, and watching butter brown can make time disappear.

Read the science

Psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi described "flow": a state of deep focus that happens when a task is challenging enough to hold your attention but not so hard that it's frustrating. Hands-on tasks with clear steps and instant feedback, like baking, are a classic setting for it.

Further reading: Csikszentmihalyi, Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience (1990).
